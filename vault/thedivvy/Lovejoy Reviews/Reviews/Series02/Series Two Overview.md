# Title

Series Two — The Divvy Verdict

# Subtitle

Eleven episodes, a higher floor and a lower ceiling. Series Two spent six weeks looking like the better run and then quietly stopped being it.

> One without ever being as good", which was true at six episodes and is not

## The argument

Here is the thing I did not expect to find when I added the scores up, and then the thing I did not expect to find when I added them up again two months later.

Six episodes in, Series Two was running at 4.5 against Series One's 4.22. On that basis it was the better run, and I said so repeatedly, in the reviews themselves.

Eight episodes in, Series Two is running at **4.19**. Series One finished on **4.22**. It has gone past me in the opposite direction while I was still saying nice things about it.

Two episodes did that. *National Wealth* at 3.5 and *Sugar and Spice* at 3 — the first time this run has produced anything I would actively skip, and it produced two of them back to back.

So the argument needs restating rather than abandoning, because most of it still holds.

**The ceiling is still lower.** Series One has **four** fives: *The Judas Pair*, *The Sting*, *Friends, Romans and Enemies* and *The Firefly Cage*. Series Two has two, and both of them landed in the first three weeks.

**The floor is still higher, but only just.** Series Two's worst is now a 3. Series One's worst is *Death in Venice* at 2.5, with *To Sleep No More* at 3 — so the gap between the two floors has shrunk from a chasm to half a star. What I cannot say any more is the thing I was leaning on hardest: that Series Two has yet to make anything I would skip. It has now made two.

**And the shape is different, which is the part I had not noticed.** Series One's two bad episodes sit in the middle of the run and it recovers. Series Two's are consecutive, at episodes seven and eight, and I do not yet know whether that is a dip or a decline. Three episodes left to tell me.

Which leaves the honest version: for six weeks Series Two was the better run, and then it stopped being it. Consistency is the thing you notice when it is gone — and it went at exactly the point I had finished praising it for.

## What is driving it

A few things, and they are all the same thing really: this run knows what it is.

**It stays put.** Suffolk and Essex, pubs, cottages, auction rooms, a market square. The one episode that reaches — <Ep>The Italian Venus</Ep>, with its two-million-pound bronze and its baronet — is the joint-lowest scored of the six, and the two that go smallest, *Bin Diving* and *Who Dares Sings*, are the two I liked most. There is a lesson in that which the programme itself appears to have learned faster than I did.

**The crew is the show.** Every score in this series turns out, when I read the reviews back, to be a judgement about who was in it. *Bin Diving* got a five despite having no Tinker and I said at the time I had no defence for that. *The Italian Venus* got docked a star for the same absence a fortnight earlier. *Montezuma's Revenge* got half a star back purely because everybody was present. *Who Dares Sings* was a five until a costume decision. I appear to be scoring the cast list.

**Gimbert is gone and it shows.** Charlie Gimbert is in Series One and then not seen again until Series Four. Two entire series without him. The reviews keep mentioning it, which is usually a sign that something load-bearing has been taken out.

## The ranking so far

*Eight of eleven. This will move — three episodes to come, including a
feature-length finale with Linda Gray in it, which could land anywhere.
Renumber as they land; the finished list will be a top eleven.*

*Note that the six entries below the two new ones did not need renumbering:
National Wealth and Sugar and Spice both score under everything that came
before them, so they slot straight in at the bottom. That is the whole story
of the back half in one bookkeeping detail.*

**8. Sugar and Spice** — [[S02E08 - Sugar and Spice]] · 3/5
A schoolgirl turns up at the barn with a folio of eighteenth-century erotica
and the episode never works out whether it is a caper, an argument about art,
or a blackmail case about a teenage pregnancy. It runs all three at once and
lands none of them. The first ten minutes — Lovejoy, Jane, Susan and Vicky in
one marquee — are as good as anything in the series, and then it goes to
London and stays there.

**7. National Wealth** — [[S02E07 - National Wealth]] · 3.5/5
A French gang stripping country houses back to the floorboards, and Lovejoy in
bed with the auctioneer when they get to his client's. Jean Kent and Ronald
Fraser are worth the hour on their own. The plot around them is thinner than
the cast deserves.

**6. The Italian Venus** — [[S02E02 - The Italian Venus]] · 4/5
The first proper sting of the nineties run, and a good one — Ian McNeice being force-fed a cake he never ordered, and a two-million-pound Venus standing in the drawing room with a bulb in its head all along. Docked a star for having no Tinker in it, a rule I then broke a fortnight later and have never satisfactorily explained.

**5. One Born Every Minute** — [[S02E06 - One Born Every Minute]] · 4/5
More fun than its position suggests, and Tom Wilkinson six years before anybody knew his face. Lovejoy and Tinker alone together for a whole hour, which turns out to be a good combination. What holds it back is what it finds charming — a man who picks out lonely women, paints their house for two hours and takes £200 and rather more besides.

**4. Montezuma's Revenge** — [[S02E04 - Montezuma's Revenge]] · 4.5/5
Complete nonsense and I will defend all of it. Three civilisations in one episode, a gold raft that looks like tat, a blowpipe in Suffolk, and a motive that turns out to be a drug debt. Held up by the whole crew being back and by Tinker getting the best speech in the series.

**3. Who Dares Sings** — [[S02E05 - Who Dares Sings]] · 4.5/5
A £300 piano propped up on a 1532 Chaucer, a doctored set of silver, and the whole gang given something to do. It was a five until somebody turned up to the party dressed as Hitler, and the missing half is entirely Adolf's fault.

**2. Just Desserts** — [[S02E01 - Just Desserts]] · 5/5
Five years away and the programme walks back in as though it had never gone. Lovejoy out of prison with a score to settle, Miriam arriving, an antique condom joke inside ten minutes. A confident return that stays almost entirely in Suffolk, having plainly learned something from Venice.

**1. Bin Diving** — [[S02E03 - Bin Diving]] · 5/5
A rubbish round in the rain, a widow with a leaking roof, and Warren Clarke running a parallel economy out of other people's bins. Nobody leaves Suffolk and nobody tries to make Lovejoy into anything he isn't. Five out of five with no Tinker in it, which I still cannot justify and still would not change.

## Notes for finishing this

- **Do not publish until all eleven are reviewed.** The `ranking` frontmatter
  drives ItemList JSON-LD, and the SEO title pattern promises "ranked, worst
  to best". An eight-of-eleven list keeps neither promise and would need
  rewriting three more times. The argument flipping between six and eight is
  the case for this rule, not against it.
- When it does go live it wants `seoTitle: "Lovejoy Series 2 episodes ranked,
  worst to best"` and `heading: "Lovejoy Series Two, ranked"`, matching the
  Series One overview.
- The route registers itself. Dropping `02-overview.mdx` into
  `src/content/series/` is all that is needed — `scripts/lib/routes.mjs`
  derives `/series/2/overview` from the filename.
- The `<Ep>` shortcode above is currently written without a slug as a
  placeholder. Fill in `series-2-episode-2-the-italian-venus` when porting.
- Running average and spread, recalculated on each publish. Computed from the
  `score` frontmatter across `src/content/reviews/`, not kept by hand:
  Series 1 — **4.22** over 9 files, spread 2.5–5.0, four fives. (Nine files,
  eleven broadcast episodes: S01E09-10 is one MDX covering both parts.)
  Series 2 — **4.19** over 8, spread 3.0–5.0, two fives.
- **The argument has already flipped once and may flip again.** At six
  episodes Series Two led 4.50 to 4.22 and the spine was "higher floor, lower
  ceiling, no duds". At eight it trails 4.19 to 4.22 and the no-duds claim is
  dead. Recompute before writing anything that quotes a number, and treat the
  subtitle as provisional until episode eleven is in.
- The floor/ceiling framing still works and is still the spine — it just needs
  the honest version, which is that the floor is only half a star higher rather
  than the chasm it looked like at the halfway mark.
- Watch the shape as well as the average. Series One's two weak episodes sit
  mid-run and it recovers; Series Two's are consecutive at seven and eight. If
  nine, ten and eleven keep sliding, this is a decline rather than a dip and
  the piece is about a run running out of steam. That is a better essay than
  the one originally planned, so it is not a problem if it happens.
