## Cold open — checked

Mat's, typeset only. Spelling and punctuation fixed, *Sugar and Spice*
italicised, nothing added or reordered. The MDX carries the ported version.

- **The Bayeux Tapestry opened at the British Museum on 10 September 2026**,
  two days before this was written, in the Sainsbury Exhibitions Gallery, and
  runs to **11 July 2027**. First time in England in roughly 900 years. So
  "has arrived" is right and "a couple of weeks" sits well inside the run.
- **"An embroidery really" is correct.** Wool yarn worked on linen, not woven.
- **The Ælfgyva panel.** Inscription reads "Ubi unus clericus et Ælfgyva" with
  no verb — the only incomplete sentence on the whole seventy metres. A cleric
  touches her face; in the lower border a naked squatting man mirrors his
  gesture. Generally read as pointing at a scandal the original audience knew
  and we have lost. Her identity is unrecoverable; Ælfgyva was an extremely
  common name in eleventh-century England.
- **The penis count — IN, at Mat's explicit request**, together with the joke
  that this is the only episode of *Lovejoy* the observation would make sense
  for. **George Garnett**, professor of medieval history at Oxford, counted 93
  in 2018: 88 equine, 5 human. **Christopher Monk** claimed a 94th in 2024,
  which Garnett disputed.

  The names are deliberately kept out of the prose — "somebody has been the
  whole seventy metres with a magnifying glass" is the register the cold open
  is written in, and naming an Oxford chair would flatten the joke. They are
  recorded here and in the MDX comment trail in case the claim is queried.

  This is also the review's amusing-unnecessary-detail quota met, which the
  house style asks for and which a short review can easily miss.

## Guest notes

**Caroline Blakiston** (b. 13 February 1933). RADA 1957, straight into the
Bristol Old Vic. Marjorie Ferrar in the BBC's *The Forsyte Saga* (1967), three
*Avengers* episodes, the ITC rounds including *The Saint* and *Randall and
Hopkirk (Deceased)*. **Mon Mothma in *Return of the Jedi* (1983)** — about
ninety seconds of screen time, and the thing she will always be asked about.
Bernadette Borsodi in *Brass*. RSC ensemble at Stratford 1993-97, which is two
years after this. Aunt Agatha in *Poldark* for the modern audience. Retired;
credits run 1961-2019.

The part needs her. Hemmingway is a snob, a liar, an institution-protector and
the person who eventually fixes it, and Blakiston plays all four without
smoothing any of them down.

**Aden Gillett** (b. 12 January 1958, in Aden, Yemen — hence the name). RADA.
*The House of Eliott* began later the same year and ran to 1994, all 34
episodes, which is what he is known for. Theatre World Award for Best Newcomer
on Broadway for *An Inspector Calls*. Here he gets a chemist with a locked
drawer and not a great deal else.

## Antique Lesson material — CUT from the published review, parked here

Researched and drafted, then cut to keep the review light. It goes back in as
an `### Antique Lesson` section immediately before `### The Divvy Verdict` if
it is ever wanted. Everything below is checked.


**Henry Fuseli**, born Johann Heinrich Füssli, Zurich 1741, died London 1825.
Royal Academician, Professor of Painting and later Keeper of the Academy. *The
Nightmare* (1781) is the famous one. He produced a substantial private body of
erotic drawings, many of his wife Sophia Rawlins, and Sophia destroyed a number
of them after his death. Over 150 of his drawings of women survive.

The script's line — "After the Marquis de Sade, Fuseli was the greatest
pornographer of the 18th century" — is the script talking, not art history.
Worth saying so rather than repeating it as fact.

**Marcantonio Raimondi** — the second-visit folio adds Fragonard, Daumier,
Géricault and Raimondi, and Raimondi has the story. He engraved *I Modi* in
1524 after drawings by Giulio Romano: the first printed erotica in Europe
published as such. Clement VII ordered the edition destroyed and had Raimondi
imprisoned. Almost nothing of the first edition survives.

**Badenside's founder is Henry Spencer Ashbee** with the name filed off.
Ashbee (1834-1900) compiled a three-volume bibliography of erotica under the
pseudonym Pisanus Fraxi and left the British Museum around 1,379 volumes on his
death. The trustees destroyed six boxes and locked away the rest as the Private
Case.

**Keep the Secretum and the Private Case separate.** Popular accounts run them
together and they are different things: the Private Case is the library
material (Ashbee), the Secretum is the object collection, largely seeded by
George Witt's 1865-66 "Collection Illustrative of Phallic Worship". What
remains of the Secretum is still catalogued as cupboard 55.

There is a real joke in this and the draft takes it: Lovejoy's speech is the
historically literate position, Hemmingway's is the institutional one, and the
two of them are re-running a two-hundred-year-old argument without knowing it.

## Plot beats

- Miriam dies in the opening seconds. Jane drives. This is why Susan's car gets
  commandeered later, and why the car keeps changing hands all hour.
- Lovejoy is six months in arrears on Vicky's fees at Badenside. Miss
  Hemmingway raises it at parents' day, in front of Jane, and again at the end:
  full payment, school ends Thursday.
- Susan is there because of a client — Cyril Harrington-Morse of Homestead
  sofabeds, whose slogan is "Pull me up a put-you-up and pick me up!" Lovejoy
  solves the case forty minutes later off that one line.
- Khadija Subhani, sixth-former, brings a bound folio of Fuseli prints to
  Lovejoy's barn. Then a second lot: Fragonard, Daumier, Géricault, Raimondi.
- She is caught coming out of the Mistresses' Library at Badenside and claims
  to be after railway timetables. "Train spotting." Lovejoy pays it off later:
  two trains a day, one to bring the station master to work and one to take him
  home.
- Lovejoy offers the Fuselis back to Miss Hemmingway, who denies they are the
  school's and is furious. Susan hears about it within the hour.
- Charles Garner takes the prints off Khadija by force and starts placing them.
  Taffy Griffiths is the fence; the trail runs Taffy → Berwick Street auction →
  Sam → back to Taffy → Garner.
- Eric bids £50 for *My Adonis* at the auction and immediately regrets it.
- The art-shop scene: Picasso's post-Blue-Period black, Braque's red, Cézanne's
  pastels and a felt tip. "If it's good enough for Picasso, it's good enough
  for me."
- Garner's buyer is Harrington-Morse. Lovejoy works it out on the doorstep.
- The break-in at the chemist's. Eric on shotguns, ammonia and sulphuric acid,
  then the radioactive symbol. "Eric, this is a small-town chemist, not Three
  Mile Island."
- Jane phones Taffy posing as a buyer looking for a birthday present for her
  husband. Taffy offers to put "by appointment" on his letterheads.
- Vicky is expelled and delighted: even Celia Finnigan couldn't manage it, and
  Celia Finnigan punched the games mistress.
- **The reveal.** Aisha Subhani, Khadija's older sister, now at Cambridge and
  engaged to Bobby Khan. Two years ago at Badenside she thought she was
  pregnant, couldn't go to the school doctor, went to the local chemist for a
  test. Positive. She had an abortion. The chemist was Garner. He read about
  the engagement in the papers and has been selling her the results back ever
  since. Khadija was stealing from the school library to pay him.
- Hemmingway kills the blackmail by giving Aisha an alibi for any week Garner
  cares to name. Then hires Lovejoy to value and catalogue the collection,
  which covers the fees, and takes Vicky back.
- Susan demands a divorce and is told she has one.
