## One Wink Plot

Lord Felsham goes into business with a property developer who smiles too much. Meanwhile, the developer's wife pawns her wedding pearls to help her son get to Australia. Lovejoy suspects the land deal and ropes Tinker in to value the necklace. One of these jobs requires a military rank.

## Review

The international break means a quiet weekend and time for the very enjoyable *Lily's Pearls*. It's the last standalone episode of a rather splendid Series Two before the double header of *Riding in Rollers* and *The Black Virgin of Vladimir*. Without giving too much away, the first half is rather superior to the second, but both are good and a massive upgrade on the [*Death in Venice*](https://thedivvy.co.uk/episodes/series-1-episode-9-10-death-in-venice/) debacle that ended Series One. We had to wait nearly five years for another episode after that.

I listened to the [*Lovejoy Actually* review](https://www.lovejoyactually.com/listen?offset=1554271200834), and they didn't think nearly as much of it as I did. Two fours and a six out of ten is more shocking than Lily's outfit! They did get me thinking about Lovejoy Bingo, though, so I made a [card you can play along with](https://thedivvy.co.uk/bingo/).

In *Lily's Pearls* we get a slightly incompetent, bewildered Lord Felsham, out of his depth in a fast-changing world he doesn't quite comprehend. And in walk Lily and Joe Gruder, a fully realised couple who seem quite at home in it. Like Lassy the dog from [the previous episode](https://thedivvy.co.uk/episodes/series-2-episode-9-raise-the-hispanic/), I could quite happily have had these two show up again. They don't.

If they ever reboot *Lovejoy*, I wonder whether Lord Felsham will try to get into AI and get bamboozled by a Jensen Huang type. The Nvidia co-founder has been in the headlines over an alleged Chinese honey trap, which sounds like the sort of thing Alexander would insist he had completely under control. Who knows, but if this ends up as an episode of the reboot, let this paragraph serve as proof I was there first. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/df45d21030691f59c67bc0b35adb89a2)

Anyway, I digress. On to *Lily's Pearls*, Series Two, Episode Ten!

### Salt of the earth

“Salt of the earth,” says Alexander when Jane asks if she'll like Joe. Not too snobbish, but almost there. “Salt's not good for you,” says Jane. Meanwhile, Joe asks Lily why she never wears her pearls any more. We're up and running before anyone's had dinner.

Miriam has broken down again, so Lovejoy arrives in Eric's sidecar. Double bingo? I may need a bigger card. Lily's worried about what to call her hosts. “It couldn't be more simple. I'm Jane, he's Alex.” Lily thought she was going to say Tarzan.

It's sweet how quickly Lovejoy and Lily get on. He's a man of the people. I love the SPQR gag and Lily's reaction to Rosemary's new horse. Fifteen hands, apparently. Lily wouldn't like to sit next to him in a cinema.

Then Lovejoy gets a divvy feeling about Joe. “There'll be tears before bedtime,” as his granny used to say. His divvying is going beyond antiques now. He's met enough villains to recognise a smile like a slit in a letterbox. Alexander gets a little nasty. Lovejoy came in a sidecar and Joe came in a Roller, so who do you suppose knows more about real business? Then Alex has a row with Jane and tells her assets are there “to be made to sweat”. This is the 1990s. Thatcherite stuff.

It's well written. I'm still deciding whose side I'm on, Joe's or Lord Felsham's. Of course, we're all on Lovejoy's side.

### Doin' up the house

Joe offers Lovejoy £50,000 to deck out the house, with £2,000 in cash for him. Lovejoy gives us a delightful wink to camera. There's the subplot and a reason to get Tinker and Eric in. Joe also wants a portrait for Lily's birthday. Of himself, obviously. He's seen the ancestors on the Felshams' walls, and Lovejoy knows just the man to take the photographs. Eric, the Bailey of Bury St Edmunds.

Alexander wants what the entrepreneur has and Joe wants the cachet of the aristocracy. It's all a bit snobbish, really. Lily favours Regency. Stripes. Dad was a Brentford supporter. Good luck turning that into a dining table. And we're less than three years on from Harry Enfield charting with *Loadsamoney (Doin' Up the House)*. He might as well have been singing about Joe. [Official Charts](https://www.officialcharts.com/songs/harry-enfield-loadsamoney-doin-up-the-house/)

Then Joe takes Lovejoy aside to tell him Jane has eyes for him. He's got all this from a faraway look over the port. Lovejoy looks straight at the camera. **This is bingo madness.** We're halfway through *Lily's Pearls* and I'd practically forgotten about the pearls.

### Wahey, the pearls are back

Joe wants them valued for insurance and he wants it done tonight. Lily's hocked them to help her son Alan get to Australia. Joe's disowned him, so she can hardly ask for the money, and the pawnbroker's in Amsterdam. After a slow set-up, we're sprinting.

Meanwhile, Eric's investigating Joe on microfiche in what looks like every eighties library I remember from childhood. This is exactly the kind of nostalgia I'm here for. He's rather good at it, too. Later it's Eric who spots the plan for Odyssey Park in the background of one of his photos.

### Favourite Moment

**Duelling jewellers.** Lovejoy talks Tinker into becoming Major Dill, jeweller extraordinaire, by hinting that Jane might think less of him if he refuses. Is this another bloody bingo? Then Joe turns up with an actual jeweller, so Tinker has to value the pearls with Howitt sitting across from him.

“Slightly pink. Almost apricot.” South-East Asia, he reckons. He goes through the lustre, works in a bit of Richard Burton and Elizabeth Taylor, then listens to Howitt value the necklace at £42,000 and goes three grand higher. This is great stuff. Afterwards he admits he couldn't tell pearls from barley sugar.

“Who's playing silly buggers, then?” says Joe, striding in. It turns out he'd sold the real pearls himself four years ago when a property deal went belly-up. Lily pawned his fakes. Joe was trying to replace them before she found out. Lovejoy goes to recover the imitation strand and has great fun pretending to be Joe. He goes all Michael Caine on “Tell me something I don't know.” McShane was in *Battle of Britain* with Caine. Maybe he picked up a little something on set. [Cast and credits](https://www.tcm.com/articles/72498/battle-of-britain)

### Good Quote

> “Twenty-odd grand them pearls cost me, and that's when a grand was a grand!”

Joe gives us the price, the history and the measure of the man in one go. And he still has to ask Lily why she never wears them.

### Guest Focus — Sherrie Hewson

Hewson gets some of the best lines over dinner, but it's the quieter scenes with Jane that make me want Lily back. When Jane tells her she can choose her own kitchen, Lily looks as if the possibility has only just occurred to her. A little later she's telling Jane she's pawned her wedding present for a son Joe won't help. By then I'd have happily watched another episode with the pair of them, even if all they did was decide on the kitchen.

### Antique Lesson

They say “paste pearls” so often that I finally looked it up. They're imitation pearls. One traditional way of making them was to coat glass beads to give them the lustre of the real thing. So I've learnt something. Will I remember it? [Gemological Institute of America](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/fall-2024-gemnews-cotton-pearl-imitations)

### The Divvy Verdict

Lovejoy and Tinker waterboard the planning chap with G&Ts and we're back on the gravel scheme. Alex thinks he's getting a mere or a bird sanctuary after they dig out the gravel. What Joe's actually applied for, with the Felsham name on it, is a boating theme park with a motel, shops and a caravan site. Lovejoy warns Alex that this carbuncle will discredit Jane. “But she's the friend, you're the husband.” Alex fires back, “I wish you'd remember that more often.”

That's one reason I like the episode so much. Jane and Alex are always at arm's length, even when they argue. Joe and Lily are the complete opposite. You can feel how much they matter to each other, and I think Jane wants a bit of that warmth herself. She finds some of it with Lovejoy. But Lily's also plainly frightened of Joe, and when he tells her he'd be blitzed if she ever left him, it lands a little oddly after what we've just seen. I can see why the podcast had its doubts.

And I think they may be onto something about the ending. It peters out a bit once the pearls are sorted. I do like the last exchange, though. Joe reckons he and Lovejoy are in the same business. Find a bit of tat and sell it as Louis Quinze. Lovejoy has a rule about conning friends, and reckons there are things you can't put a price on. “Name one,” says Joe. Cue Lily in the pool. “Best deal you ever made,” says Lovejoy, “and it didn't cost you a penny.”

So it's **four out of five**. Some of the sexual politics are a bit odd and I'd have liked a better finish, but the characters are great, the crew do some magnificent work, there are plenty of cosy pub scenes and I've got almost a full bingo card. If Lord Felsham does get into AI, I just hope Jane makes him read the planning application first.

Onwards and upwards to Brian Blessed, Linda Gray and the Series Two closer!
