## Research — settled by the transcript

**Soundtrack: no substitute needed.** The first genuinely-heard track since Bin
Diving. The Crime and Punishment cabaret number is Flanders & Swann's "A
Transport of Delight", their tribute to the London bus, from At the Drop of a
Hat (1957). Performed twice — at the opening party and again over the closing
scene. Spotify ID 0xpQun2KliPp3OWLvPSAym.

There is a **second** Flanders & Swann song in the cold open: "The Gnu Song",
on the car radio, with Tinker singing along and Lovejoy declining to. Two
numbers by the same duo, thirty-odd years old when this went out. Also briefly
sung: Irving Berlin's "This Is the Army, Mr. Jones". And Linden closes on
Scott Joplin's "The Entertainer" after Aunt May asks for "that tune that
introduces the snooker on BBC2" — which it isn't. Worth a line; check whether
the joke is the character being wrong or the script being wrong.

**Locations — the lead is now much stronger.** The pub in the episode is named
in dialogue: "Three Horseshoes, Pillbridge". Pillbridge is invented, but the
production kept the pub's real name, which corroborates Waymarking's claim
that the building is the Three Horseshoes at Hazel End. Caution: Waymarking
also lists a different Three Horseshoes, at Molehill Green, for Members Only
(S3), so the name alone does not pin the building. Safe to say the pub is
called the Three Horseshoes on screen; still only probable that it is the
Hazel End one.

**The plot, verified.**
- Jane decorates, Lovejoy furnishes, Linden pays — except Linden has spent
  everything on renovation. "Him not pay, me go."
- Linden is "Stinker", Tinker's national service mate. Three days to opening.
- Aunt May's two silver bowls, "Paul de Pomerey, 1760 ish", supposedly £40k,
  brought from Ireland in 1928 after the big house burned down.
- George Palmer is an ex-prison officer, not an ex-con — he was a screw on
  Lovejoy's landing, sat through Lovejoy's lectures on French Renaissance
  furniture, then took his pension and set up in house clearance.
- The con: Angela Moxon's sister works in a travel agency, so they know who is
  on holiday. Lovejoy pays £6,000 (£3,000 each) for furniture out of
  "Angela's" house, which is really the Gooches', who are away. "I just
  burgled a house and paid three grand for the privilege."
- **The payoff is the piano.** Lovejoy wanted a square Dutch four-legged job,
  got something else for £300, one end propped up on a pile of old books. In
  the pile: a fine copy of the second collected edition of Chaucer, edited by
  William Thynne, printed 1532. It makes £60,000 at auction. £300 in, £60,000
  out. Lovejoy gives it to Linden — "khaki being thicker than water".
- **The silver is a fake, twice over.** De Pomerey's signature was scratched
  off and the hallmark altered, because as an antique the bowls were taxable
  and as silver gilt they were not. Aunt May knew all along. Worth about
  £3,000, not £50,000. James Parrish, a London silver specialist, spots that
  the hallmark reads 1765 when de Pomerey had been dead two years.
- Crime and Punishment decoded: **Major Crime** Linden and **Corporal
  Punishment** Tinker. Linden was never brass — rank and file, and never
  denied it in thirty years. Lovejoy works it out and lets him keep it.
- Miriam is impounded: bald tyres, no MOT, no insurance — and then Adrian the
  mechanic lends her to a darts team for an away match.
- DI Dennis Hardwick gives Lovejoy a week and takes Palmer and Angela
  red-handed. Closing gag: Lovejoy already knew which house they would do.
- Aunt May moves in with Linden permanently and will not wash up.

**Cross-episode catch.** Palmer asks Eric whether he has never heard of
superglue. One episode after Archie superglued a pre-Columbian gold king to
his dashboard in Montezuma's Revenge. Two weeks running.

**Quote candidates.**
- Jane, to Palmer, after he calls her crumpet: "Crumpet, as far as I'm aware,
  is a cake one cooks on a griddle." Probably her best line in either series.
- Jane again: "I fight without armor, as you may well discover before much
  longer."
- Lovejoy on Linden: "Bonkers, broke and an old buddy of Tinker's. Not exactly
  the best credentials in the world."
- Lovejoy: "I just burgled a house and paid three grand for the privilege."
- Lovejoy: "Major Crime Linden and Corporal Punishment you!"
- Eric, on Lovejoy borrowing £20 on Friday morning to make up his wages on
  Friday evening: "My mum says it's robbing Paul to pay Paul."
- Lovejoy: "What does go on under that beret?" Tinker: "Memories, Lovejoy.
  Memories."
- Aunt May: "I'd rather go back to Windsor, to loneliness and the danger of
  being attacked. Or having a fall."

**Jane and Lovejoy.** Alexander away again — "You do loosen up once young
Alexander goes away, don't you?" And the quiet one: Jane visited Lovejoy in
prison, because "perhaps it's the one place I felt safe with you", and both
she and Alexander knew he was innocent. That is a straight continuation of
the Janey thread from Montezuma's Revenge.

## Research — verified before watching

- First shown Sunday 3 February 1991, BBC1, 20:05, 55 minutes (TVmaze).
- Written by Douglas Watkinson. Directed by John Woods. Both new to the site.
- Premise: Lovejoy and Jane are hired to fit out a smart new restaurant for
  Major Linden Walker, an old army friend of Tinker's. With help from a former
  prison officer Lovejoy delivers the goods, then finds he has been gulled.
- **Tinker gets a storyline.** He and Linden have a cabaret act, Crime and
  Punishment, making its first and last appearance. After no Tinker at all in
  The Italian Venus and a cellar full of drink in Montezuma's Revenge, this is
  the most he has had to do in Series Two.
- The title puns on the SAS motto, Who Dares Wins, which the army thread sets
  up. Note the title is punctuated "Who Dares, Sings" by TVmaze and TMDB, and
  "Who Dares Sings" by Wikipedia. Slug drops the comma either way.

## Guest notes

- **John Wells (1936-1998)** — guest star. Satirist, actor and writer. Co-wrote
  Private Eye's "Mrs Wilson's Diary", then originated the "Dear Bill" letters
  with Richard Ingrams from 1979, purporting to come from Denis Thatcher. Built
  it into the West End farce Anyone for Denis? (1981), playing Denis himself
  opposite Angela Thorne. Two Bond films: Fordyce in the 1967 Casino Royale,
  and Denis Thatcher in For Your Eyes Only.
- **Peter Armitage (1939-2018)** — Bill Webster in Coronation Street across
  three spells (1984-85, 1995-97, 2006-11), having already played a different
  character there in 1977. Before acting: engineering apprentice, then the
  Merchant Navy. In 1980, after a season at the National, he toured Australia
  13,000 miles by motorbike, busking with a guitar. Good unnecessary detail.
- **Eleanor Summerfield** — veteran of British film and television from the
  forties onward.
- **Robert Austin plays a character called Bruce Wayne.** No explanation
  available. Batman had been in cinemas eighteen months earlier (June 1989),
  so it may not be innocent.
